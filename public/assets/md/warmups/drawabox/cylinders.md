## Construction around a minor axis

1) Start with a **long minor axis line**, longer than the cylinder.
2) Place an ellipse towards one end, but not right on the end.
3) Place a second ellipse towards the other end, **smaller and with a wider degree**, assuming this is the farther end of the cylinder.
4) Connect the ellipses to form the cylinder.
5) Looking closely to each ellipse drawn, check for errors by extending their minor axes towards the cylinder's vanishing point.

## Construction inside a box

1) Draw a box using the Y method. Try your best to keep the planes containing the ends of the cylinder **square** in 3d space.
2) Find the **center** in 3d space of both of the square ends by drawing from corner to corner.
3) Draw a line that passes through the centers of both planes, representing the minor axis.
4) Draw an ellipse in the closer and farther end of the box, trying to get it to touch all four edges of the plane, while keeping it aligned to the minor axis.
5) Draw the sides of the cylinder.
6) Check for errors by extending the boxes' edges, the minor axis of the ellipses, and the contact points between the ellipses and the edges of the plane containing them.

## Mistakes to avoid

### Lines parallel on the page
> Make sure all of the boxes' lines and cylinders converge.

## Goal

The goal of this exercise is to get used to drawing freely rotated cylinders without explicit vanishing points, and to draw cylinders inside boxes to reinforce the understanding of 3D space.
