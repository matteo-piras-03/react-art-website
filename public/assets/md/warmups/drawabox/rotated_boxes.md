1) From now on up to step 3, use a ruler. Draw two perpendicular axes.
2) Draw a square centered on the axes, ensuring its sides are parallel to the axes.
3) Place another square at the end of each axis. These squares serve as reminder as to the fact that boxes are being rotated across a full 180 degree arc.
4) **Putting the ruler aside**, draw the rest of the central box.
5) Draw a neighbouring plane, by copying the same one as the neighbour.
6) Draw the rest of the box, making sure **it gets rotated** compared to the neighbouring one.
7) Repeat this process for the rest of the boxes.

## Mistakes to avoid

### Not actually rotating the box
> Whenever placing another box to the side of another rotated box make sure that the new box actually rotates by moving the vanishing point.

### Not keeping things together
> Keeping the gaps narrow allows for neighbouring forms to be used as effectively as possible.

### Not drawing through boxes
> The exercise will be cleaner but it will be worth less of what you get out of it.

## Goal

This exercise removes the direct consideration of vanishing points, and brings the attention back to the things we're actually drawing, which exist within the page or canvas we're working on.
