1) Establish a center flow line.
2) Add small ellipses along the line, making sure to **align their minor axis** and varying their degree.
3) Draw a segment from the first ellipse, **past the second and halway to the third**.
4) Keep doing this for the whole branch.

After enough practice, you will find yourself being able to manage two or three segments at a time.

>**Optional for warmups**: You can fork branches by adding two ellipses close together, drawing a ball around said ellipses, drawing an ellipse on one side of the ball and a flow line that represents the new branch path.

## Mistakes to avoid

### Drawing the whole edge in one stroke
> Make sure to draw the edges of the branch with multiple strokes, as drawing with one stroke can easily make you lose control or drawing stiff and wobbly.

### Visible tails in a compound stroke
> Try to minimize the visible tails in each segment, as this is the ultimate goal of the exercise. Every stroke needs to flow smoothly to the next one.
